## Summary
Adds the read-only cash report (spec 0002: pure domain in `src/domain/caisse.ts`, `caisse` module with detail and summary routes, three indexes) plus a `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` contract lock on payment and withdrawal, with retrait and concurrency integration tests. The report is well built: totals come from the returned lines, a single RepeatableRead snapshot, Benin-day arithmetic is correct, scope rules are right. The headline risks sit around it: the lock leaves the advance path and the idempotent-replay path inconsistent, and a field-supplied date can hide a movement from every report.

## Major
### 🟠 Idempotent replay can return 422 for a movement that was recorded, `src/modules/collecte/collecte.service.ts:31-38` (same in `retraits.service.ts:39-46`)
**Problem**: The idempotency lookup happens before the transaction and the lock. Two simultaneous requests with the same `id` (phone retry after a timeout while the first is still in flight, or sync push overlapping a direct call): the second waits on the contract lock, then re-reads aggregates that already include the first movement. It then fails with a business rule (exceeds the target, contract no longer ACTIF, withdrawal above recoverable) before reaching the unique violation that triggers the P2002 replay branch.
**Why it matters**: The client receives 422/conflict for a payment or withdrawal that is in fact recorded. Sync reports a conflict, the agent may re-enter it: money collected twice or a withdrawal seen as refused while the cash left. Directly against the zero-discrepancy goal. Before the lock this was a race; now the lock makes the losing path deterministic. No test covers concurrent same-id requests.
**Suggested fix**: After taking the lock, look up `idempotencyKey` again inside the transaction and return the existing row. Add a concurrency test with the same `id` sent several times in parallel (all 201/200 with the same row, one movement in base).

### 🟠 Advances are not locked, so the lock does not close the cash race, `src/modules/avances/avances.service.ts:35`
**Problem**: Withdrawal reads advances (`avanceRestante`, `interetDu`) and the new lock only serialises payments and withdrawals. `accorder` takes no lock: advance versus withdrawal (wrong net and interest), and two advances (same `rang = count + 1`, `maxAvances` exceeded) can still interleave. Spec 0002 Follow-up admits it, and the concurrency suite is silent on it.
**Why it matters**: Advance is cash leaving the agent's pocket; an advance and a withdrawal computed on a stale picture produce a cash report that cannot be reconciled with contract state. The change ships a "contract lock" that reads as complete.
**Suggested fix**: Call `verrouillerContrat` first in the advance transaction (same pattern, plus the in-transaction idempotency re-check) and add the advance cases to `concurrence.int-spec.ts`; or, if deferred, track it as a dated scope item and state the gap in `verrou-contrat.ts`'s doc comment.

### 🟠 A movement dated in the future is invisible to every report, `src/modules/caisse/caisse.service.ts:71-80` (root cause: `encaisser.dto.ts` / `effectuer-retrait.dto.ts` accept any `paidAt` / `dateRetrait`)
**Problem**: The day of a movement is the field-supplied date, with no bound. A phone with its clock ahead (or a typo) files cash under a future day. `resoudreJour` refuses future days with 400, so that movement appears in no report until that day arrives, and no `arriveeTardive` or other flag shows it.
**Why it matters**: Cash received today is absent from today's balance with no signal, exactly the unexplained discrepancy the pilot must not have. The spec lists the bad phone clock as a tradeoff, but the "no signal" part is the dangerous half and the fix is cheap.
**Suggested fix**: Reject (or clamp and flag) a `paidAt`/`dateAvance`/`dateRetrait` later than now plus a small tolerance at write time, or at minimum count and return "movements dated after the generation instant" in the report. Promote the follow-up about phone-clock drift before pilot.

## Minor
### 🟡 Lock can be taken on any contract id before the ownership check, `src/modules/collecte/collecte.service.ts:38`
An AGENT can queue a lock on someone else's contract by uuid before the 404, and lock waits are bounded only by the Prisma transaction timeout (default 5 s, surfaces as a 500 on contention). Consider mapping lock/timeout failure to a retryable 409/503 and checking ownership without a lock first.

### 🟡 Synthesis reads the whole day with no pagination or bound, `src/modules/caisse/caisse.service.ts:186-240`
Fine at pilot volume (documented), and the new composite indexes lead with `agent_id` so the all-agents query (`paid_at` range only) cannot use them. Revisit with a `(paid_at)` index when volume grows.

### 🟡 Scope check runs after date resolution, `src/modules/caisse/caisse.service.ts:85-86`
An AGENT asking for another account with a future date gets 400 instead of the 404 AC-9 prescribes. Harmless but easy to reorder.

### 🟡 Concurrency test 4 asserts only the final invariant, `test/integration/concurrence.int-spec.ts:121-150`
It passes whatever order wins, which is correct, but the first three tests rely on a real race to fail without the lock; no deterministic proof (e.g. hold the lock in one transaction and assert the other waits) that the lock is what protects.

## Nits
- ⚪ `src/modules/caisse/caisse.service.ts:96-170`, the `lignes` mapping repeats the select/projection three times; a small helper per type would shorten it.
- ⚪ `src/common/verrou-contrat.ts:14`, `contrats` is hard-coded in raw SQL; a rename via `@@map` would silently break it (the integration tests would catch it).

## Strengths
- Totals are computed by one pure function over the same lines that are returned, single RepeatableRead snapshot, and summary equals detail by construction (AC-4, key invariants).
- Benin-day maths is pure UTC arithmetic with no server-timezone dependence; boundary, leap-year and invalid-date cases are covered.
- Integration tests cover AC-2 (attribution), late arrival, negative balance, soft-deleted member and agent, deterministic ordering, and role scope.
- The lock helper is small, documented, and placed first in the transaction, with invariant-based assertions (sum in base, not only HTTP codes).

Configured. Domain (`caisse.spec.ts`), DTO spec, 21 caisse integration tests, 9 retrait tests and 4 concurrency tests cover the new logic well. Gaps: concurrent same-`id` replay (Major above), advance concurrency, and the future-dated movement case.
